      Help

      Setup

      OCR Lite needs the model (1.4 GB) in this browser's storage — the setup checks the device and downloads it once from Hugging Face. An installed app (Home Screen, Dock, Chrome / Edge app) has its own storage: set it up inside the app you use.

      The model is LightOnOCR-3-1B · Historic German Handwriting (open, CC BY-SA 4.0). About the project, the output format and the source: the project page and GitHub (questions and reading errors worth reporting: its issues).

      Workflow

      1. Open an image, or drop it on the image pane.
      2. The tools come in three rows: Crop (what part of the image), Adjust (how it looks to the model), Read. On a phone's side the first two share a line.
      3. Crop row: turn the image, then Page or Auto. Page puts the whole image in a rectangle, Auto the text block it finds. Drag the rectangle's edges or corners to adjust it, inside it to move it, then ✓ (Apply); ✕ cancels. On a cropped image Page and Auto start again from the uncropped one. Undo and back to the original at the row's end.
      4. Adjust row: Contrast for faint ink, Pencil for uneven paper — each is on or off, a second press takes it out.
      5. Scale (Adjust row): how tall the letters come to the model. Without a crop the image is read as is. On a cropped image the slider sets the letters' height in px — from 9 to 45 px (the model learned from letters 11 … 43 px tall), and never past the model's 1540 px input; when the slider cannot reach 9 px, crop tighter. Every crop change starts at 1:1 (the crop at its own size). The wand (Auto scale) scales the letters into the trained range (1:1 when they already lie in it) — a close-up made smaller, tiny writing larger; it is offered again after each crop change or slider move. Beside it: the scale to the model and the letters' height there; the Read row shows the image (or crop) → the model input's size.
      6. Read — the text streams in; Stop ends the read at once (the text so far stays).
      7. Edit the text: the format buttons, ▨ for illegible, Markup for the raw markup. Fit (on by default) shows every line as written, the text scaled to the panel's width.
      8. Save: the markup (.txt), the rendered page (.html) or the model's input image (.png).

      Phone, and tablet upright

      Image and Text are two tabs (a bar at the bottom; on a phone's side after the logo); each keeps its zoom and scroll, and shows only its own tools. A new image opens on Image, a read switches to Text as its text begins.

      Zoom

      − / + / fit above the image; ⌘ / ctrl + wheel or a trackpad pinch; two fingers on a touch screen. A drag pans; a double-click or double-tap fits. The text zooms the same way (two fingers, ⌘ / ctrl + wheel); Fit starts it again from the whole line width.

      Status line

      load the model's weights onto the GPU (each read) · image the page encoded · prefill the prompt read · tok/s the reading speed. "hit the token limit" = the read ran away: crop tighter or try another preset. "stopped: the app went to the background" = switching apps stops a read; read again.

      Offline

      Once downloaded, the model stays in this browser's storage and OCR Lite reads without a connection. Settings shows the stored files; deleting them frees the 1.4 GB.

      Devices

      A read takes about 1.8 GB of GPU memory, given back when it ends: iPad and Mac with Apple silicon are fine; iPhone is experimental (tested: iPhone 16 Pro, 43 tokens/s; iPhone 18 Pro, 80 tokens/s). On iPhone and iPad the device check also tests the memory, before the download. Chrome / Edge 113+, Safari 26+.

      Check this device tests the GPU (WebGPU, its limits, a 1-second speed test) and estimates the reading speed. A laptop with two GPUs may run the browser on the slower built-in one — the check then says how to switch to the graphics card.

      Tech preview

      Reading in the browser with WebGPU is work in progress; on phones and tablets OCR Lite is not production-ready yet. Edits are not kept: a crash or a reload loses the text on the right — copy or save what you change. Real editing, with the work kept, is the next major step. A read interrupted by switching to another app starts again from the beginning when you come back.

      Privacy

      Everything runs on this device: images and texts never leave it.
