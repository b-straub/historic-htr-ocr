Outlook

The first release reads the well-represented hands well and the rare ones less so. These are the directions worth pursuing — no roadmap or dates.

Adapting to a new collection

A model that already reads 19th-century hands should need only a few pages to learn a new one. A first test points that way: 28 transcribed pages of an unseen, hard notebook and under an hour of training halved its CER (44 % → 25 %). One collection is not a rule — the next step is to repeat it on other hands and to make it a simple recipe an edition project can run on its own pages.

Hard hands

Humboldt's late hand (28 % CER on the validation pages) and the Nägeli letters (13 %) show where the model is weakest; unknown hands range from near-perfect to hard. More ground truth for such hands, and the few-shot route above, are the levers.

The format

  • Double underlining (〔〔…〕〕) is in the format and the converter; the model learns it with the next training round.

More open sources, a freer licence

The model is CC BY-SA 4.0 because some of its training texts are. Licence clarifications with the ShareAlike sources are in process; once they are settled, a later release may be CC BY 4.0. Editions and archives that publish transcriptions with their scans openly are the material this project depends on — more of them means a better reader for everyone.

Editions and archives that want to help: the most valuable contribution is an openly licensed corpus with a diplomatic TEI transcription and its scans — every such edition makes the reader better for everyone. Pages that read badly are welcome too. Please open an issue.

Code

The sources will follow here step by step, as each part is cleaned up — no dates yet:

  1. OCR Lite — the browser app and its engine (Rust, WebGPU), the first and smallest step.
  2. The converter and the metric — convert_output.py (already on the model page) and the oCER code.
  3. Data and training tooling — from the editions to page ground truth, and the training setup: the largest part, and the one that needs the most polishing before it is useful to others.

Until then, the model page on Hugging Face describes the data, the training and the evaluation.
