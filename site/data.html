Whose hands

83 % of the training pages come from German-speaking Switzerland. The Escher letters alone are 62.5 % of the pages — but many hands, not one: 76 % of those pages are letters to Escher from 702 named senders, Escher's own letters are 14 % of all training pages. French is about 8 % of the pages, Latin and English under 1 % each.

From an edition to page ground truth

The editions publish their text for reading — usually TEI, sometimes PAGE XML from Transkribus — not for training a page reader. Each source therefore has its own small extractor that turns its files into one page of ground truth per scan, all under one rule set:

  • Page order, not text order. Margin notes, insertions written apart, later stamps and numbering become their own blocks where they stand on the page; footnotes and catchwords stay at the foot (reading order).
  • As written. The edition's expansions, normalisations and editorial notes are dropped; abbreviations, spelling and line breaks follow the ink. Strikes, insertions, underlines, super- and subscripts become the inline marks.
  • Defects are flagged, not guessed. A page where the edition omits text, where the text under-fills the page or a catchword is unread is left out of training.
  • Cropped to the sheet. Every scan is cropped to the paper edge (one page of a double spread) and must pass a crop check before it is used.
  • Reviewed samples. Page samples of every source were checked by hand against the scans.

Checked and unchecked ground truth

530 of the 610 validation pages have checked ground truth — an edition's transcription or hand-corrected Transkribus pages. The other 80 are unchecked: 65 Basel land-register pages are automatic HTR (PyLaia), 15 German 20th-century pages are Transkribus transcriptions not marked as corrected. They are scored, but they do not decide the checkpoint.
