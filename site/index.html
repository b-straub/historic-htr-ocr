Reading historic German handwriting, a whole page at a time

An open model that reads pages of German Kurrent and Latin-script handwriting, mainly of the 19th century, and writes them as written — line by line, with struck-out, inserted and underlined text marked. It runs in your browser, on your own device.

Try it in the browser The model on Hugging Face See an example

Why

Archives and libraries hold millions of handwritten pages that nobody can search, and fewer people every year can read Kurrent. A first transcription — not an edition, but a draft that follows the page — makes a collection findable, lets a reader check a passage against the scan, and saves an editor from starting at zero. This project trains such a reader only on openly licensed editions and archives and gives it back openly.

The model

LightOnOCR-3-1B · Historic German Handwriting is a fine-tune of LightOnOCR-3-1B (LightOn): one page image in, the whole page out, with no line segmentation step before it. On 610 held-out validation pages from 11 sources it reads at 4.7 % CER (3.85 % oCER, our order-tolerant metric); the stock base model reads the same pages at 45 %. Hard hands stay hard — see the model card for every figure, the limitations and how to run it with Transformers, vLLM or llama.cpp.

Try it: OCR Lite

OCR Lite is a small web app that runs the model entirely on your device with WebGPU: the model (1.4 GB) is downloaded once from Hugging Face into the browser's storage; your images never leave the machine. Open a scan, crop it to the page, read, correct the text, save it as text or HTML. It needs Chrome / Edge 113+ or Safari 26+ (Mac, iPad; iPhone experimental) and about 1.8 GB of GPU memory; a device check tells you before the download whether your machine can run it. A tech preview: edits are not kept yet — copy or save your text.

Examples

And one page from outside all training material: a letter of Johann Wolfgang von Goethe to his son August, 25–29 June 1830 (Badische Landesbibliothek Karlsruhe, K 703, Public Domain Mark). Against a transcription corrected by the author, the model read it with 18 character errors on 1,333 characters.
